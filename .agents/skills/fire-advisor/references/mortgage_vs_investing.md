# Mortgage Strategy: Overpaying vs. Investing in F.I.R.E.

This guide provides the mathematical, structural, and behavioral framework to evaluate whether a UK client should overpay their residential mortgage or invest the surplus capital.

---

## 1. The Core Mathematical Comparison

### Guaranteed After-Tax Return vs. Expected Return
* **Mortgage Overpayment**: Provides a **guaranteed, tax-free return equal to the mortgage interest rate** $r_m$.
  * If your mortgage rate is 5.0%, overpaying £10,000 saves £500 in interest per year. This is equivalent to earning a guaranteed 5.0% net yield.
* **Investing in Equities**:
  * Historic long-term nominal return of global equities (MSCI World / FTSE All-World): ~7.5%–8.5%.
  * Historic real return (after inflation): ~5.0%.
  * Net expected equity premium: $\approx 2.5\% - 3.5\%$ above mortgage debt over 15+ year horizons.

---

## 2. The Tax Wrapper Arbitrage Factor

A critical mistake in traditional advice is comparing mortgage rates directly to general investing while ignoring UK tax wrappers.

### Case A: Higher Rate Taxpayer (40%) Choosing Between Pension vs. Mortgage
* Available pre-tax gross income: £10,000.
* **Option 1 (Overpay Mortgage)**:
  * Income tax (40%) and NI (2%) apply $\rightarrow$ Net cash in hand = £5,800.
  * Overpay mortgage at 5.0%: Saves **£290/year** in interest.
* **Option 2 (Salary Sacrifice / SIPP Contribution)**:
  * Full £10,000 enters the pension tax-free.
  * Grows at 7.0%: Generates **£700/year** gross.
  * Even after future tax in retirement (25% tax-free, 75% taxed at 20% basic rate), net terminal wealth is over **70% higher** in the pension.
* **Verdict**: For 40% and 60% taxpayers who have not exhausted their annual pension allowance, **pension contributions mathematically crush mortgage overpayment**.

### Case B: Basic Rate Taxpayer (20%) or ISA Funding vs. Mortgage
* When funding is directed toward a Stocks & Shares ISA:
  * Net cash available: £8,000.
  * Mortgage rate: 5.5%. Guaranteed return = £440/year.
  * ISA expected equity return: 7.5% nominal. Expected return = £600/year.
  * The risk premium is narrow (2.0%). In high-interest rate environments ($>5\%$), overpaying the mortgage becomes much more competitive on a risk-adjusted basis.

---

## 3. Impact on F.I.R.E. Target Calculation

The single biggest F.I.R.E. advantage of clearing a mortgage before retirement is the **structural reduction in required portfolio size**.

### Scenario: Retiring with vs. without a Mortgage
* Client spends £30,000/year on living expenses + £15,000/year on mortgage = £45,000 total.
* Target SWR = 3.5% (multiplier = 28.57x).
* **With Mortgage**:
  $$\text{FIRE Target} = £45,000 \times 28.57 = \mathbf{£1,285,650}$$
* **Mortgage Paid Off**:
  $$\text{FIRE Target} = £30,000 \times 28.57 = \mathbf{£857,100}$$
* **Net Reduction in FIRE Portfolio Needed**: **£428,550**!
* By eliminating the fixed debt obligation, the client permanently lowers their baseline expense floor, reducing Sequence of Returns Risk during early retirement.

---

## 4. The Practical F.I.R.E. Decision Matrix

| Client Profile / Market Condition | Strategic Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Higher / Additional Rate Earner (£50k–£125k+)** | **Prioritize Pension/Salary Sacrifice**. The 40%–60% instant tax relief far exceeds any mortgage interest savings. |
| **Mortgage Rate $< 3.0\%$ (historic fixed rate)** | **Do Not Overpay**. Invest surplus in ISAs / high-yield cash; earn higher yield while maintaining total liquidity. |
| **Mortgage Rate $> 5.5\%$ (variable / remortgage cliff)** | **Hybrid Strategy**: Maximize pension up to higher-rate relief boundary, then split remaining surplus 50/50 between ISA and mortgage overpayments. |
| **Within 2–3 Years of Early Retirement Date** | **Accelerate Debt Elimination**. Entering retirement debt-free drastically narrows spending variance and shields against SORR. |
